# Overview of ACAPS Risk Analysis and Methodology

ACAPS' risk analysis methodology aims to provide humanitarian decision-makers with forward-looking insights by assessing potential future events that may have significant humanitarian consequences. The methodology helps in identifying and analyzing risks, enabling improved preparedness and response. ACAPS primarily focuses on **risk analysis** and **scenario-building**, both of which rely on structured frameworks to assess potential future hazards and their impacts. However, ACAPS does not engage in **forecasting**.

## Key Principles of ACAPS Risk Analysis

1. **Contextual Understanding**: Effective risk analysis requires a solid understanding of the current context and the interplay of variables influencing change.
2. **Ongoing Process**: Risks should be analyzed regularly to track changes over time, improving accuracy and insights.
3. **Severity and Probability**: The overall risk is determined by both the potential severity of an event and its probability of occurring.
4. **Preparedness Focus**: The analysis provides early warnings, helping to shape preparedness measures for potential crises.

## Core Concepts and Terminology

- **Risk**: A combination of the probability of a hazard and the estimated humanitarian impact.
- **Hazard**: A potential event or situation with negative humanitarian consequences (e.g., conflict, natural disaster).
- **Impact**: The number of people affected, the intensity of their needs, and the population's vulnerability and capacity to cope.
- **Probability**: The chance of a hazard occurring, expressed as a percentage or rating.
- **Exposure**: The population at risk of being affected by a hazard.
- **Vulnerability**: The susceptibility of individuals or communities to be affected by hazards, considering physical, social, and economic factors.

## Risk Analysis Methodology

ACAPS’ risk analysis follows a structured six-step process:

1. **Situation Analysis**: A comprehensive review of the current situation, vulnerabilities, and response capacity.
2. **Identification of Hazards**: Pinpointing potential hazards that may arise in the given context.
3. **Estimation of Humanitarian Impact**: Assessing the possible number of people affected (exposure), the severity of the impact (intensity), and the vulnerability of those affected.
4. **Probability Assessment**: Estimating the likelihood of each hazard materializing.
5. **Risk Profiling**: Categorizing risks as low, medium, or high based on impact and probability scores.
6. **Monitoring and Revision**: Regular updates to the risk assessments as the situation evolves.


## Risk Impact and Probability Estimation

- **Impact Calculation**: Combines the metrics of exposure, intensity, vulnerability, and coping capacity to estimate the potential severity of a hazard.
- **Probability Estimation**: Utilizes subjective judgment, as most humanitarian hazards cannot be precisely quantified, with probability expressed on a five-point scale.

## Risk List Methodology

The equation used for determining risk is: Risk = Impact x Probability


- Hazards are scored based on exposure and intensity, with corresponding levels of impact.
- The probability is then factored in, assigning the final risk level (low, medium, or high).

### Selection Criteria for Risk Profiling:

- **Low Risk**: Ignored unless a compelling reason exists.
- **Medium/High Risk**: Written up in a risk profile and continuously monitored.
- **Emerging Risks**: Focus on new, escalating risks that could lead to increased humanitarian needs.
